# Ground Without the Trees

A digital elevation model is a grid of heights. Each cell holds one number: how high the ground sits above some reference level, usually sea level. That is all it is. No colours, no hills drawn by hand. Just a field of numbers that a map can be painted from.

I want to see whether I can say this without the page open. So here it is, plainly. Take a square of land and cut it into small squares. Measure each one. Write the height down. Repeat until the whole place is a table. Lay the table out as an image and the bright cells are high and the dark cells are low. Your eye does the rest.

The awkward part is what the numbers mean. A DEM is usually a surface, not the bare earth. A radar or laser pass can hit the top of a canopy or a roof before it reaches the ground. Some models are cleaned up to show the ground alone, and some are left raw. Which one you have changes every answer you get from it. I keep forgetting that, and then remembering it at the worst moment.

Cell size matters too. A coarse grid smooths a ridge into a bump. A fine grid shows the bump's edges but also its noise. Neither is the truth. Each is a choice about how much to forget.

So: a grid of heights, read carefully, with the question of what the heights are standing on always in mind. I think I can say that now. I will check again tomorrow.
