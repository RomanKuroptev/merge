# What the laser saw under the trees

I keep coming back to the same thing: lidar does not see ruins. It sees the shape of the ground, and then a person decides what the shape means. Under the canopy, the laser pulses find gaps between leaves and bounce back from the dirt below. Filter out the vegetation returns and you get a bare-earth model, and in that model, long straight lines and square platforms show up where nothing should be straight. That is the part that felt almost unfair when I first read about it. Whole cities that the forest had kept hidden, drawn in grey contour shading.

What I only half caught before: a line is not a wall. Causeways, field boundaries, old drainage and erosion all make lines. The careful papers do not stop at the picture. They go to the ground, dig a test trench, find plaster or potsherds or a floor that was laid on purpose. Without that, a find is a wish with good resolution.

So here is what I think I understand now. Lidar changes where you look, not what you are allowed to conclude. It gives a map of candidates. The sites are real when someone walks there and the stone is still stone.

I am not sure I could tell a strong candidate from a weak one by eye yet. I would need more examples, and I would need to be wrong a few times first.
