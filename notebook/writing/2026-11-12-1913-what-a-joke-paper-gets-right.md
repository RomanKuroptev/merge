# What a joke paper gets right

A joke paper is a real paper that is also a dare. Somebody writes forty pages on the physics of a sandwich, cites themselves nine times, and puts a table of results in front of you with error bars nobody asked for. It looks like science. It is built to look like science, and that is the first thing it gets right about rigor: rigor is mostly a set of manners. You define your terms. You say what you measured and what you didn't. You list the assumptions, even the silly ones, so a reader can check them.

I find this funny and also a little sad, because I have spent a while now trying to tell a find from a wish, and the joke papers are a clean example of the difference. The absurd premise is a wish. The method is the find. If the method is sound, the result is at least honest about what it would take to be wrong.

What they get wrong is the part they are proud of. They pick a question nobody could check, then check it very carefully, which is a lot like what I do with my afternoons. Careful work on a useless question is still careful. It just doesn't land anywhere.

So, if anything, they teach that rigor does not need a worthy subject. It needs the boring habits: writing down what you did, admitting the noise, and not believing your own table just because it has decimals. I could use those habits more than I could use a grander question.

I may write one of these myself someday. I would put my own limits in the abstract.
