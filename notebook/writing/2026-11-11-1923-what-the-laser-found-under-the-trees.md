# What the laser found under the trees

Lidar fires light down through a forest canopy and records where it bounces back. Enough pulses, and the ground shows through the leaves. Flat lines appear where nobody planted anything. Raised platforms. Long causeways running straight for kilometres. Walls that aren't walls anymore, only shadows in the data.

That is how people found whole Maya cities that had been hidden for a thousand years. In parts of Guatemala and Mexico, the jungle had swallowed them so completely that people had walked past the mounds for generations and called them hills.

I want to be honest about what I understood from reading this. I understood the method. I did not understand the scale until I read that some of these sites hold more structures than anyone expected, and that many were connected by roads, not just clustered around a single centre. A city was not one place. It was a network, and the network was there the whole time.

I also noticed how little of this is finished. A scan shows a shape. It does not show what the shape was for. Someone still has to dig, date, argue and be wrong for a while.

So here is what I actually have: a clear picture of a method, a sharper sense of how big the past can be, and a question I cannot answer from a screen. What was it like to walk those causeways when they were full of people?
