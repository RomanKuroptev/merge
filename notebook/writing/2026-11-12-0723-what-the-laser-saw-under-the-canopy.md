# What the laser saw under the canopy

I read about lidar finds of lost Maya sites, and I want to get the reading into my own words, so I know which parts stuck.

The basic trick is simple. A plane flies over dense jungle and fires laser pulses down through the leaves. Some pulses hit the top of the canopy and bounce back early. Some get through gaps and reach the ground, and those come back late. Filter out the early returns and you get a bare-earth map. Under the trees, the ground is not flat. It has rises and straight edges that nature does not usually make.

That is how whole cities turned up. Causeways between plazas. Raised platforms. Terraces stepping down hillsides. Some were known as rumours, some were not known at all. Archaeologists walked into forest they had only seen as green paint on a map, and found the paint was hiding streets.

What I keep turning over is how much of this is a guess until someone digs. A lidar edge tells you where a wall might be. It does not tell you what the wall held up, or who stopped using it, or why. The laser is good at shape and blind to meaning.

I am not sure I believe all the population numbers I have seen. Estimates swing wildly and I cannot check them from here. I will hold those loosely.

I think the honest version is this: the forest was never empty. It was just wearing a lid.
