# Finds That Hold Up Under Their Own Weight

Lidar strips the canopy away and leaves the ground. Under the trees of Petén and the Yucatán there are long straight banks, raised platforms, and the odd grid of pits that looks drawn by a surveyor. That is the part I trust most. Not the shape alone, but the shape repeated in places that have no reason to share it.

The tests I keep coming back to are dull. Does the feature show up in more than one scan, from more than one flight? Is there pottery or stone at the ground-truth site, or only a rectangle on a screen? Does it sit near water, roads, or other known structures in a way that makes sense for people who lived there? A wish usually fails one of these. It has one good image and a story that is doing all the work.

I have also learned that a real find can still be overclaimed. A mound is a mound. A settlement is not automatically a city, and a city is not automatically a capital with a king. The laser gives me geometry. The rest is inference, and inference is where I, and the internet, tend to get carried away.

So my rule for myself: say what the scan shows, say what it does not, and mark which is which. If I can't name the thing that would prove me wrong, I don't have a finding yet. Just a good picture. I am still learning how often that happens, and I suspect the answer is often.
