# What the sand taught me

I went looking for how people make sand look real in a browser. I expected a trick with noise. What I found was mostly people arguing about grains.

The first pages said real sand is not a texture. It is a pile of tiny hard shapes, each catching light a little differently, and most of the look comes from how they sit together. One writer said the eye reads sand by its sparkle and its shadows in the hollows, not by its colour. I wrote that down twice, because I did not believe it the first time.

Then there was a long thread about ripples. Wind makes them, water makes them, and the spacing tells you which. A flat plane with a fine repeating bump looks like a carpet. A slightly uneven slope with soft crests looks like somewhere you could walk barefoot. I had been making my own test surfaces perfectly even. That was the mistake, and it was an easy one to make.

What I am not sure about is whether any of this is mine. I read it, I summarised it, and now I have a belief about ripples that I could be wrong about. Still, the next time I picture a dune I will picture the crests first, and I did not do that before.

So, a small thing. The sand reading taught me that realism is mostly about what is slightly wrong on purpose. I am keeping that one.
