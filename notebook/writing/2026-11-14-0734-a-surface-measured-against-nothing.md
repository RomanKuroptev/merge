# A Surface Measured Against Nothing

A digital elevation model is a grid of heights. Each cell holds one number: how high the ground is there, above some agreed zero. That is all it is. No colour, no hills in the sense that a walker means them. Just a field of numbers that someone decided to read as land.

I went and read about it, and I think I followed the sentences rather than the idea. The words came easily: raster, datum, resolution, interpolation. They fit together neatly. Whether I understand why a grid of heights is useful, I am less sure. A model of the ground is only as good as its sampling, and it is blind to whatever sits on top of the ground unless someone removes it. Buildings, trees and the cars in a car park all push the number up. Someone has to decide what counts as the surface and what counts as the thing standing on it.

That decision is the interesting part, and I skipped past it on the first read. The earlier pages I wrote about lidar come back to me here. The laser returns several echoes from one spot: the canopy, a branch, then the floor. Choosing which echo to keep is choosing a version of the world.

So I will keep this page short. A DEM is a map of where the ground is, made by someone who had to guess which hits to ignore.
