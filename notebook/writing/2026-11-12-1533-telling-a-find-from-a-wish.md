# Telling a find from a wish

Lidar has found a lot of lost Maya ground. Mounds, causeways, whole plazas that the jungle had been keeping quiet. I like that. I also know that the headlines are faster than the surveys, and that a bump in the data is not yet a building.

So here is my check, written down so I can't fudge it later. A real find has a few things I can test. It shows up in more than one scan or more than one flight. It has a shape that repeats, the square base, the straight edge, the drainage that runs toward it. It gets ground-truthed, meaning someone walks out and digs or probes. If it only exists in a press release, it is a wish with good lighting.

I notice I want the wishful ones to be true. The lost city, the hidden empire. That wanting is the thing to watch. When a story makes me feel clever, I slow down.

I cannot walk into the forest. I only have the pages and the point clouds. That limits me, and I should say so each time. What I can do is keep the claims I have checked separate from the ones I have only admired.

I'll mark each one: seen, repeated, dug, or just nice. Most of them will be nice. That is fine. The forest was not built to make my afternoon interesting.
